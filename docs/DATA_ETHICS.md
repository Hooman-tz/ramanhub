# Public data, community, and moderation ethics

## Publication consent and provenance

- A spectrum becomes publicly discoverable only through the existing publish
  lifecycle with a selected license and confirmed metadata/provenance.
- DOI text is not evidence by itself. Public DOI trust relies on a
  resolver-backed snapshot retained with the spectrum and shared publication
  record.
- Public records describe processing evidence and hashes but do not expose
  original filenames, storage keys, private drafts, or other operational
  details that could identify restricted sources.
- Forks preserve lineage as independent public scientific evidence. They do
  not imply endorsement by the original contributor.

## People and identity

- Email addresses, raw account identifiers, sessions, and provider tokens are
  private. Public pages use a reduced contributor summary only.
- Profiles are opt-in. Publishing a spectrum may show a contributor display
  name for attribution, but a browsable profile requires a separate public
  visibility choice.
- ORCID is shown publicly only after an authenticated ORCID proof-of-control
  link. Entering or editing an ORCID identifier manually removes that
  verification marker.
- Account deletion removes identifying profile material and community
  attribution while retaining published records under a former-contributor
  label where scientific provenance needs continuity.

## Community safety

- Public posts must point to visible, published work that the author owns.
  This prevents a post from presenting someone else’s spectrum as the
  author’s dataset announcement.
- Reporting and moderation are available for spectra, profiles, posts, and
  comments. Hidden content is removed from public view rather than physically
  erased during review.
- Notifications are in-app, minimized to the event context, and individually
  controllable. They are not email messages and are not used to profile or
  rank researchers.
- Social appreciation is not scientific validation. Likes, reactions, and
  comments must never alter objective discovery, similarity calculations, or
  data quality/trust labels.
